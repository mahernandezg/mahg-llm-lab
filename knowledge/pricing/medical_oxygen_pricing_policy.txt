Medical Oxygen Pricing Policy – Hospital Contracts

Medical liquid oxygen pricing is typically governed by long-term contracts and strict regulatory frameworks.

1. Contract Structure
Most hospital agreements include:
- Fixed price per ton
- Minimum annual volume commitment
- Indexed adjustment clause (energy index or CPI)
- Tank rental agreement
- Maintenance service agreement

Pricing flexibility during emergencies is limited.

2. Regulatory Constraints
Medical oxygen is a critical healthcare product.
Price increases during emergencies may be:
- Legally restricted
- Contractually prohibited
- Ethically sensitive

Margin recovery during emergency situations is often not allowed.

3. Volume-Based Pricing
Hospitals are often segmented by:
- Annual consumption
- ICU capacity
- Strategic importance

Higher volume hospitals receive:
- Lower base price per ton
- More favorable logistics rates

4. Emergency Delivery Pricing
Emergency deliveries:
- May not carry surcharge in regulated markets
- Often absorb additional logistics cost internally
- Reduce margin per ton

5. Cross-Subsidization Risk
If industrial contracts are interrupted to prioritize medical supply:
- Industrial penalties may apply
- Margin mix shifts negatively
- EBITDA volatility increases

6. Pricing Governance
Financial controller and commercial director must approve:
- Any deviation from contracted price
- Emergency discount or surcharge decisions
- Temporary contract renegotiations

7. Strategic Pricing Considerations
Medical oxygen supply supports:
- Reputation
- Long-term customer retention
- Public trust
- Government relations

Pricing decisions must consider long-term strategic value, not only short-term margin.

Emergency situations require balancing:
Patient safety
Regulatory compliance
Financial sustainability
