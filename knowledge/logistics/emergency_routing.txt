Emergency Routing and Distribution Contingency – Liquid Oxygen (LOX)

In a hospital liquid oxygen distribution scenario, emergency routing refers to the reconfiguration of transport and delivery plans in response to production stoppage, demand surge, or logistics disruption.

A production plant shutdown (planned or unplanned) may create supply imbalance across regions. To prevent tank depletion (dry-out), the following emergency routing strategies must be implemented:

1. Cross-Plant Rebalancing
- Activate alternative production plants within the network.
- Reallocate tanker fleet capacity to support affected regions.
- Prioritize medical oxygen over industrial contracts if required by regulation.

2. Dynamic Route Optimization
- Recalculate delivery routes in real-time based on:
  - Current tank levels at hospitals
  - Consumption forecast
  - Distance to nearest available production source
- Use telemetry and GPS fleet tracking systems to reduce response time.

3. Priority-Based Allocation
- Classify hospitals by criticality level (ICU capacity, emergency load, trauma centers).
- Deliver first to high-risk facilities with <48h storage autonomy.

4. Emergency Buffer Deployment
- Deploy mobile cryogenic tanks where fixed storage is insufficient.
- Increase temporary storage capacity in high-consumption hospitals.

5. Fleet Surge Capacity
- Activate standby drivers and vehicles.
- Authorize overtime under emergency operations protocol.
- Coordinate with third-party transport providers if internal fleet is insufficient.

6. Regulatory Compliance
- Ensure ADR (transport of dangerous goods) compliance.
- Maintain full traceability of emergency dispatch decisions.

Emergency routing decisions must be supported by real-time telemetry, predictive demand models, and a centralized operations control center.
