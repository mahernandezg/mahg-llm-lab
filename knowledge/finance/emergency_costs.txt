Financial Impact of Emergency Oxygen Deliveries – Industrial Gas Company Perspective

Emergency oxygen deliveries significantly impact cost structure, working capital, and profitability.

The main financial impacts include:

1. Increased Transport Costs
- Emergency routes are longer and less optimized.
- Fuel consumption increases.
- Overtime wages for drivers and operations personnel.
- Use of third-party transport providers at premium rates.

2. Production Cost Surge
- Running backup plants at suboptimal efficiency increases energy consumption per ton.
- Rapid ramp-up increases maintenance wear and tear.
- Possible penalties from interrupted industrial contracts.

3. Working Capital Pressure
- Higher inventory requirements (safety stock increase).
- Increased receivables if hospitals delay payment.
- Increased payables for urgent supplier procurement.

4. Margin Erosion
- Emergency deliveries may not be priced at premium due to regulatory constraints.
- Medical oxygen pricing is often contractually fixed.
- Cost per delivered ton increases while revenue per ton remains stable.

5. Risk Provisions
- Financial controller must evaluate need for contingency reserves.
- Insurance deductibles may apply if interruption triggers contractual penalties.

6. KPI Impact
- EBITDA margin reduction.
- Increase in cost per ton-kilometer.
- Decrease in logistics efficiency ratio.
- Increased operational risk exposure.

Emergency events must be analyzed using scenario-based financial modeling to quantify impact on:
- Monthly P&L
- Cash flow forecast
- Cost allocation per hospital
- Return on capital employed (ROCE)

Financial governance requires full traceability of emergency cost drivers and post-incident review.
