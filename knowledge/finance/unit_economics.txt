Unit Economics – Medical Liquid Oxygen (LOX)

Unit economics for medical liquid oxygen distribution must be analyzed on a per-ton and per-ton-kilometer basis.

1. Revenue per Ton
Revenue per ton is typically contract-based and fixed for hospitals under long-term supply agreements. Pricing often includes:
- Base LOX price per ton
- Delivery fee
- Tank rental (if applicable)
- Service fee

2. Cost per Ton (Production)
Production cost drivers include:
- Electricity consumption (cryogenic air separation is energy intensive)
- Maintenance of compressors and distillation columns
- Depreciation of plant assets
- Labor
- Regulatory compliance costs

Energy typically represents the largest variable cost component.

3. Logistics Cost per Ton-Kilometer
Distribution costs are highly sensitive to:
- Distance to hospital
- Fleet utilization rate
- Fuel cost
- Driver labor
- Loading/unloading time
- Return-empty distance

Low fleet utilization increases cost per delivered ton significantly.

4. Marginal Cost vs Fully Allocated Cost
Marginal cost includes:
- Energy
- Incremental labor
- Transport fuel

Fully allocated cost includes:
- Depreciation
- Overhead
- IT systems
- Control center operations
- Corporate support functions

Emergency events increase marginal cost significantly but also impact allocation efficiency.

5. Safety Stock Impact on ROCE
Increasing safety stock:
- Improves service level
- Increases working capital
- Reduces Return on Capital Employed (ROCE)

Financial controllers must balance:
Service Level vs Capital Efficiency

6. Key Financial KPIs
- EBITDA per ton
- Cost per ton delivered
- Cost per ton-km
- Working capital days
- Plant utilization rate
- Fleet utilization rate

7. Impact of Emergency Events
During emergency routing:
- Cost per ton-km increases
- Energy cost per ton may increase due to plant ramp-up inefficiencies
- Overtime increases labor cost
- Third-party transport increases logistics premium

Unit economics analysis must support scenario-based decision making.
